# SU2 — Card Summary & Briefing Writing Guide

**Owner:** Sunny
**Est. Time:** 30min
**Dependencies:** None

---

## Card Summaries

Cards are short — one topic, one takeaway. The reader should understand the point in under 5 seconds.

### Example 1 — AI News

| | Text |
|---|---|
| **Bad** | "OpenAI has announced a new multimodal large language model with enhanced reasoning capabilities and improved performance benchmarks across a variety of tasks including coding, mathematics, and natural language understanding." |
| **Good** | "OpenAI released a new model that's significantly better at math and coding." |

**Why bad:** Jargon-heavy, padded, buries the point.
**Why good:** One sentence. Anyone can grasp it instantly.

---

### Example 2 — Business/Markets

| | Text |
|---|---|
| **Bad** | "Macroeconomic headwinds and tightening monetary policy have resulted in a contraction of venture capital deployment across the technology sector in Q1 2026." |
| **Good** | "Startups are raising less money right now — investors are being more cautious." |

**Why bad:** Reads like a finance report, not a morning briefing.
**Why good:** Translates the idea into plain language without losing the meaning.

---

### Example 3 — Science & Health

| | Text |
|---|---|
| **Bad** | "A peer-reviewed study published in Nature Medicine suggests correlational evidence between sleep duration and cognitive decline risk factors in adults over 60." |
| **Good** | "A new study links poor sleep to higher dementia risk in older adults." |

**Why bad:** Hedged to the point of being unreadable.
**Why good:** Clear subject, clear finding, no unnecessary qualifiers.

## Briefing Prose

The briefing is the spoken/written intro that stitches the cards together. It should feel like a smart friend catching you up — warm, clear, and quick.

### Opening

| | Text |
|---|---|
| **Bad** | "Good morning. Today's digest contains a curated selection of top stories across technology, business, and global affairs, aggregated from multiple reputable sources." |
| **Good** | "Good morning. Here's what's worth knowing today — a big AI release, a market shift, and one story you'll want to share." |

### Transition between topics

| | Text |
|---|---|
| **Bad** | "Moving on to the next category of news, we have some developments in the geopolitical sphere." |
| **Good** | "Switching gears — things got interesting overseas overnight." |

### Closing

| | Text |
|---|---|
| **Bad** | "That concludes today's AI-generated news briefing. Have a productive day." |
| **Good** | "That's your 10 minutes. Go make something happen." |

---

## Rules of Thumb

1. **Write for ears, not eyes.** If it sounds unnatural read aloud, rewrite it.
2. **One idea per card.** If you need a comma, consider splitting it.
3. **Cut the qualifiers.** "A study suggests a possible link" → "A study links."
4. **Avoid jargon unless your audience lives it.**
5. **Be specific.** "A major tech company" is weaker than "OpenAI."
