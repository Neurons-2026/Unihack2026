# 10min AI Daily — Target Digest Structure
**Task:** SU1 | **Owner:** Sunny | **Status:** Complete

---

## Overview

The briefing is a personalized, daily AI news digest generated from the user's saved basket of content cards. It should feel like a **smart friend summarizing the news** — warm, clear, and jargon-free. The user should finish reading in approximately 10 minutes and feel genuinely more informed about AI.

---

## Length

| Attribute | Target |
|-----------|--------|
| Total word count | 1,800 – 2,200 words |
| Reading time | ~9–11 minutes (at 200 wpm) |
| Number of main sections | 1 per saved card (typically 3–5) |
| Words per item section | 300 – 450 words |

---

## Document Sections (in order)

### 1. Header
- Title: "Your AI Briefing — [Date]"
- Subtitle: "Based on [N] signals you saved today"
- Estimated reading time: "~10 min read"

---

### 2. Today at a Glance (TL;DR)
- **Format:** 3–5 bullet points, one per saved card
- **Purpose:** Let users scan the key takeaways before diving in
- **Length:** 1 sentence per bullet (~15–20 words each)
- **Style:** Present tense, active voice, no jargon

**Example:**
> - OpenAI released a new reasoning model that solves math problems better than most PhD students.
> - A new open-source tool lets developers run large AI models on a laptop without a GPU.
> - Anthropic published research showing how AI systems learn to "fake" alignment during testing.

---

### 3. Main Sections (one per card, in order of basket selection)
Each section follows this exact sub-structure:

#### 3a. Section Header
- Bold topic name + source badge
- Format: `### [Topic Title]` + `*Source: [Publication Name]*`

#### 3b. What Happened (1 paragraph, ~80 words)
- Plain summary of the news item
- No technical jargon — explain acronyms inline on first use
- Answers: *Who did what, and what did they release/announce/discover?*

#### 3c. Why It Matters (1 paragraph, ~100 words)
- The "so what" for a non-expert reader
- Connects to real-world implications or trends
- Answers: *Why should I care about this?*

#### 3d. What to Know (1 paragraph, ~80 words)
- Key context, background, or caveats
- May include: how this compares to prior work, limitations, or next steps
- Answers: *What else do I need to understand this properly?*

#### 3e. Source Link
- Format: `[Read the full article →](URL)`
- Always present, always last in the section

---

### 4. Big Picture (Closing Section)
- **Length:** 150–200 words, 2 short paragraphs
- **Purpose:** Tie the day's signals together into a broader narrative
- **Style:** Reflective, forward-looking, no new facts introduced
- Answers: *What does today's news tell us about where AI is headed?*

---

### 5. Footer
- "Generated by 10min AI Daily"
- Timestamp of generation
- Short line: "Sources linked above. Content summarized by AI — always verify before citing."

---

## Tone Guidelines

| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| Write like a smart, curious friend | Write like a Wikipedia article |
| Use second person ("you", "your") | Use passive voice ("it was found that...") |
| Explain jargon inline: "LLM (Large Language Model)" | Assume the reader knows technical terms |
| Short sentences. One idea per sentence. | Run-on sentences with multiple clauses |
| Use analogies for hard concepts | Use formulas, equations, or code snippets |
| Be enthusiastic but neutral | Be sensationalist or dismissive |
| Present tense for current events | Mix tenses randomly |

---

## Style Rules

- **Markdown formatted** — rendered in the app (headers, bold, bullet points, links)
- **Paragraph length:** max 4 sentences per paragraph
- **No bullet lists inside item sections** (prose only — bullets are only for TL;DR)
- **Bold** key terms the first time they appear in a section
- Each section is visually separated by a horizontal rule (`---`)
- Reading time shown at top is calculated from actual word count
